# The ring on a vine

The ball's manacle no longer bites a vine link as it bites a rock (a design decision, 2026-09-10): it THREADS onto the vine as a ring, the cord through its bore, and is a cross between a rail and a viscous face (`lib/vineClamp.ts`, `RopeVineClamp`).
Like a rail in what holds it: the ring is constrained to the cord until the player lets go of the chain or it slides off the vine's free bottom end - it cannot be pulled off sideways however the ball swings, and a span, having no free end, never lets it off at all.
Like mud in how it moves: it creeps along the cord under the chain's pull by the viscous law (`creepSpeed`, a power of the load) rather than a rail's Coulomb cone, so a hanging ball draws it slowly down the vine, a falling ball caught on one drags it a long way before it is slowed to a hang, and a ball swinging out sideways barely moves it.
Only the ball's chain does this; the player's grapple still grabs a link as it always did.

**Where the ring stands is a SEGMENT of the vine's polyline and a FRACTION along it**, the polyline being the anchor, the link centres in order, and a span's second anchor.
Not an arc length, because a hanging vine's joints run to `VINE_TOLERANCE` and its arc breathes with them, while a fraction of a segment stays put.
The chain's end contact is on the LINK at the lower end of that segment, offset to the ring's centre, so `updateVineLoads` finds the link the ring hangs from exactly as it found the link a bite was on, and rebuilds the load rope - born at the measured arc, as always - when the ring crosses into the next segment.
Once a frame, before the path is read (`Rope.settleVineClamp`), the contact is re-derived from the segment and fraction on the current polyline, so the ring rides the cord as the vine swings rather than staying where its link's frame was.
The offset gives the pull a lever on the link, and a link's spin means nothing (every constraint on it acts at its centre), so the length solve is told the ring's link has no rotational freedom (`Rope.getDynamicBodyState`, the spring body's own statement) and the pull moves it by translation alone - the light body in series that `session-225f` warns of, answered by taking away its one meaningless degree of freedom.
The ring is a PUSH FIT on the cord, its centre on the line and square to it, rather than the tilting, resting ring a rail carries; what it keeps of the rail's ring is the end the chain leaves over (`rimSign`, with `RIM_FLIP_LEAN`'s hysteresis), which both renderers face the drawn cuff by and the slack drape is pinned to (`ringEnd` is the one question the renderers and `SlackChain` ask of either kind of ring).
A strike threads on at the point of the line nearest the CUFF's own position when it struck (`BallHook.anchorTo` now puts the hook there before the owner is told), not the bite point: a link's grab circle is fat on purpose (`LINK_GRAB_RADIUS`), and the surface point of it nearest the cuff stands up to a grab radius from the cord, on the segment below the link struck.
And it threads on no nearer a free end than its own reach: a throw straight up a vine from under it meets the bottom link's grab circle from below, which projects onto the very end of the cord, and a ring on the very end of a rope is off it on the next pull - so it catches the tip of the cord instead, and slides off that.

**The creep is the mud's seat with two differences the line forces** (`Rope.slipVineClampedEnd`, at the top of every iteration of `correctShapePositionAndRotation` beside `slideClampedEnd` and `slipEmbeddedEnd`).
Only the pull's component ALONG the vine drives it, and a creep along the vine relieves only that component's share of the over-length: `slipDistance` takes an `along` cosine and solves `s = dt·creepSpeed(along·M·(e − along·s)/dt²)`, bit for bit the mud's arithmetic at 1.
The budget is spent as a walk along the polyline (`RopeVineClamp.creep`), crossing from link to link, stopping a ring's reach short of a closed end (the anchor, a span's far bolt) and running off an open one, and no further than the over-length lets, since past that the chain is slack and pulling on nothing.

**"No further than the over-length lets" is the over-length itself, and dividing it by `along` blew the game up** (`session-322f`, 2026-09-11, the first play of the ring).
The bound was written as the first-order answer to "where does the chain go slack" - move the ring `s` and the path shortens by `s·along`, so stop at `over / |along|` - and that quotient is unbounded exactly where the design says the ring should barely move, a pull square to the cord.
The winch is what brings the two together: it draws the ball up until the chain is wound around it and the ring sits on its surface, so the span reaching the ring is a couple of centimetres long and lies square to the vine, and its direction is noise that flips sign between passes.
At `along = -0.086` a 45 mm over-length bought a **522 mm** creep, which crossed three segments, turned the tangent under the ring and made the path 498 mm **longer**; the solve re-asked on the bigger error until the ring stood at the vine's top 1.6 m from the node pulling it and the path read 3.48 m against a 0.29 m constraint.
The length solve is entitled to that, so it hauled the ball 1.54 m in one frame and the next paid it back as 33 m/s - the ball, the vine and all seventeen of its links thrown across the level.
So the cap is `over` and nothing is divided: a creep of `s` relieves at most `s`, and the sideways ball is already answered where it belongs, in the budget, since `slipDistance` takes `along` and the viscous law makes the creep small.
The less efficient the creep, the *less* the geometry can be trusted over the distance - which is the opposite of what the quotient said.

The mud's other bound, `reach`, is deliberately **not** borrowed, and trying it is what showed why the two seats differ: the mud's pin creeps along the pull, so running past the node it is drawn toward is meaningless, while a ring creeps along the LINE, square to that pull as often as not.
Capped by it, the wound-tight ball closes the span to nothing and throttles with it precisely the creep that would relieve the error, which the solve then takes out by hauling bodies instead: 347 mm of up-walk and an 18 m/s ball on `ring-square`, worse than the bug.
And because the line BENDS - crossing a link turns the tangent under the ring - no bound computed at the ring's own segment can *promise* a finite creep shortened the path, so the last word is the measurement and not the estimate: a creep that did not relieve is put back (`RopeVineClamp.restoreState`, the run-off it claimed included) and its budget left to the pass after it, which looks from wherever the rest of the solve has since moved the vine to.
Before the fix that creep was kept and its `relieved` handed on as **negative** relief, which is how one bad pass became fifteen.
A ring driven off the free end is recorded and acted on at the next frame's first look (`onVineRunOff` → `BallPlayer.dropFromVine`): it comes back as the dangling tip at the ring's own centre, at the speed it was being driven at - the largest refusal in the frame, not their sum, since every iteration asks for the same budget again - and ARMED, as a cuff out of mud is, with the vine it slid off shed (`shedPieces` of its links), because the tip leaves inside the last link's own grab circle and re-caught there would thread on at the end and slide off again next frame.

The tension it reads is the other difference, and it took two attempts.
The link the ring stands on is a body the length solve may move, but it is held ON the vine by the load rope and the pair chains, which that solve cannot see: read with the link in the effective mass, a 3.75 kg link against a 52 kg ball put the tension at a fourteenth of the truth and the squared law made two hundredths of the creep.
So the link is left out of the sum (`effectiveInverseInertia`'s `except`), and a ball under a held vine reads as a ball under a fixed anchor.
That alone still read a third short on a dead hang (2.0 mm of over-length at the first look against the 3.2 the ball's fall and the creep come to, measured), because between one frame's last pass and the next frame's first look the link falls under its own gravity and its vine's pre-sweep leaves it sagging within the joints' tolerance, and the re-seated ring comes down with it; the coupled sweep lifts the link back and corrects the ball for the rest, but by then the budget was decided, and the ring crept at 1.3 cm/s.
So the first look is FLOORED by what the chain carried last frame: the length solve's summed correction impulse over every pass of the frame (`frameCorrection`, the position-impulse both ends of a rope feel whatever the split, and which the sweep's passes sum to the ball's whole correction), plus the over-length the creep relieved before it (`frameCreepRelief`).
On a steady hang that is the ball's fall exactly, and the ring creeps at 3.2 cm/s against the law's 3.0; on a catch the first look is the larger by far and stands, so the ball is slowed over the frames the slip takes to decay rather than in one (`ring-catch`: 7.1 m/s at the catch, 5.9 a frame later, 45 cm of vine in twenty frames against a centimetre of hang).
A new end clears the history, since what the chain carried to a hook in flight says nothing about the ring it has just become.

**On a stiff span the chain is a row of the span's island, and the creep is solved inside the row's velocity solve** (`viscousRopeImpulse` in `BallChainRow.solve`; why the hold moved to the island is in [vines](vines.md#spanning-vines)).
The row is a viscous slip rather than a rigid length: the ring creeps along the cord at `creepSpeed(|along|·T)`, every metre it walks takes `|along|` of a metre out of the path, so the length the bodies may open is the creep's relief, and the row solves for the tension that makes the two agree, `slackRate + |along|·creepSpeed(|along|·λ/h) = 0`.
It is `ropeImpulse` with that relief added to the rate it drives to zero, the same softness and bias, and the root is found by bisection between no tension and the rigid row's answer.
The ring then walks by the tension the substep's integration was made with (the biased pass's), over one substep, at the next substep's `prepare` or the frame's `finishFrame` (`Rope.rowCreep`), keeping only the positional creep's monotone guard.
On that row the winch is a rate, not a length: the drum's take-up over the substep about to be solved (`BallChainRow.reelRate`, the spool rate at the ball's present turn) is subtracted from the rate the row drives to zero, as a velocity solver runs any motor.
The rigid row hands the take-up over as position error for the soft bias to correct, which the biased pass pays as tension and the relax pass takes back, and with a creep reading the biased pass that split the forces: under a wind-up on `session-171f` (2026-10-13) the biased pass pulled 2-5 kN on 30-44 mm of error, the relax pass left the ball 0.4-1.5 kN, and the ring crept by the larger figure, so winding felt far less resisted than falling, which Tris reported; hanging vines never had it, since the positional solve shares one correction between the take-up and the creep.
As a rate the two passes agree within a few percent, the chain stands 1-4 mm over while winding as it does while falling, and the tension the ring creeps by is the tension the ball is reeled by (22.4 m/s² against the ball's 22.0 along the pull at the height of that wind-up).
Read before the step and not booked after it, because booked after the fact every frame's last substep of take-up carried into the next frame as ten millimetres of error.
This is how a velocity solver carries any velocity-dependent force - Box2D's friction and motor rows are the same shape - and it means there is no separate reading of the tension for the creep to get wrong: the tension is the one that lets the ball lengthen the path at the creep's relief, whatever is driving it, weight, a swing's centripetal load, the winch, or momentum.
Two more things a row frame has to do for the ring that a positional frame gets from `settleVineClamp`: the contact is re-derived onto the polyline every substep (`Rope.rowPrepare`), since left where the link's frame carried it the contact drifted off the line and jumped back with the next creep's sync, millimetres read as relief the creep had not earned or as a walk refused for a path it had not lengthened; and the row keeps its warm start across the links the ring crosses (`BallChainRow.sync` keys the end on the vine, not the link), since dropped at every crossing the creep stalled a frame per link, the ball ran on into a taut chain and was caught at twice the weight - a hitch every twenty centimetres of slide.
It replaced five drafts of 2026-10-13 that walked the ring outside the solve and told the row afterwards, each from a reading of the tension that one of Tris's sessions then showed wrong.
Bounded by the over-length the row left standing (the positional creep's bound, copied), the ring crept at a sixth of the law and stood still for thirty frames at a time (`session-256f`): the island leaves a millimetre of over-length or none.
Walked once a frame after the solve, the frame's creep was a centimetre of slack the ball fell into and was caught from, a jerky slide and a shudder under a hanging ball (`session-346f`).
Walked from last frame's tension, the ring ran ahead at the bottom of a swing, the chain went slack, the next frame read no tension, and the ball was caught at 2.5 kN, alternating at 30 Hz (`session-113f`).
Walked by the positional law read at a substep's scale, the step was a tenth of a millimetre and a wind-up with the chain along the cord did not slide at all (`session-129f`).
Bounded by a reconstruction of the ball's free motion, a ball with momentum along the cord and no winch stood still (`session-121f`), because the row had already taken that momentum off the ball before the reconstruction read it.
Measured after the change: `ring-span` creeps at 19.5 cm/s against the law's 16.3 with the chain within 8 mm of taut and every walk the way the pull leans, and wound tight it walks under a millimetre in ninety frames; replayed, `session-121f` carries the ring 20 to 35 mm a frame through its swing on momentum alone, and none of the five sessions has a frame where the tension drops to zero mid-slide.
What remains after the bottom of a swing is the law: creep goes as the square of the tension, and a pendulum's tension peaks at the bottom of its swing and drops below the weight toward the top, whichever way the chain points.

**How viscous a vine is to the ring is authored per vine** (`VineData.viscosity`, in the vine panel beside the stiffness; default `DEFAULT_VINE_VISCOSITY`, 1, the reference mud, so a ring under a hanging ball creeps at exactly the speed a cuff creeps through `ball.json`'s mud; 0 is a ring that never slides).
A number and not a flag for mud's reason: a vine a ring slides down in two seconds and one it rides for a minute are different puzzles.
Dimensionless, so `scaleLevelData` leaves it alone, and the editor writes it only where it is set.
`ball-steer` pins its vine at 0, because the ball winches itself up the chain for ten seconds there and under that load a ring crept the 60 cm to the free end and off it inside the sweep - the mechanic working, in a case about something else.

`cli vines` carries the coverage: `ring-walk` (the creep as arithmetic on a built vine - across links, stopped a reach short of the anchor, off the free end with the refused drive reported once, and a span closed at both ends), `ring-hang` (a hanging ball draws the ring down at the law's speed, never off the line, the ball descending with it, until it slides off the end as the dangling tip, does not re-catch the vine, and bites the floor it falls to), `ring-swing` (a ball swung 1.3 m out to the side never pulls the ring off the line), `ring-catch` (above), `ring-square` (the bound above: the ball winched onto the ring until the span closes to nothing and the pull comes square, then swung, and the ring must work DOWN the cord rather than walk up it, with the path on its constraint and the ball not thrown), `ring-span` (the row above: a ring on a stiff span at `ball.json`'s stiffness and viscosity creeps at the law's speed for the weight's share along the cord, only ever the way the pull leans, with the chain taut, and wound tight it stands), and `format` (the number through px→m, the editor and the bare round trip).
`cli vines --only NAME` runs the cases whose name starts with NAME.
`ring-square` is worth a word on how it is tuned, because it was green on the broken bound twice before it bit: the failure needs a BUDGET as well as a square pull, and the budget is earned by the swing, so the kick is at 9 m/s and the case reads 352 mm of up-walk, an 11.4 m/s ball and 83 invariants against the old cap.
A gentler swing is a case that tests nothing, which is why the scenario claims (the span closing, `|along|` coming down) are asserted before the bound ones.
`cli viscous` `viscous-law` pins the `along` arithmetic: square to the line no creep, at 60° less, along it the mud's own bit for bit.
The first play of it was `session-322f` (2026-09-11) and it found the creep bound above on the first go, which is the argument for playing a mechanic before trusting the cases around it: `ring-walk`, `ring-hang`, `ring-swing` and `ring-catch` were all green through the whole of that bug, because none of them winches the ball onto the ring and a ring hanging under a ball never turns its pull square.
The ring cases throw the cuff up at an angle that clears the vine's lower links, and that angle is tied to the cuff's collision bar: when the bar widened to the leg iron's band (d1124b1, 2.2 to 4.4 px) the old throw caught the last link and threaded on at the free end, and `ring-hang` and `ring-swing` went red on their setup alone until the throw was steepened (`ASIDE`, 2026-10-18).
The neighbouring throws are recorded beside it: `ring-swing` reads just over its 5 mm on the frames after the ring crosses a link (re-seated once a frame, it hangs off its new link with a full segment's lever), and `ring-square`'s thrown-ball bound is aim-sensitive, so its green is not a fix of the open creep-cap gap.
`ring-span`'s wound-tight bound is priced from the tension the row carried rather than the weight: a ball wound tight is still swinging under the ring, and once the drum stopped slipping (5366eb8) it read a ring following the law as a wander.

**A hanging cord the ring holds is a row of its own island** (`ringUnit` in `syncSpanUnits`, 2026-10-18), as a stiff span always was: the cord becomes a `PlantUnit` the frame the ring threads on, the chain a `BallChainRow` of it (`syncBallRow`'s existing gate, the ring's link now being tied), and the cord goes back to the sweep the frame the ring lets go.
Positional, a ball whirled on a light rope barely moved it (`session-175f`, "barely moving even though it's not very heavy"): the spin rollback took the ring link's share of every correction back (the whirl's winding pins the share at 1; 430 mm solved onto the grab link over 131 frames, 332 taken back), so the ball was whipped round a grab that hardly answered.
Two positional answers were measured and reverted first.
Letting the link keep its share, as a plank or berry does, left the coupled sweep unconverged: the light link took ~91% of each correction, the vine pulled it back, the chain ended each frame ~4 mm over with the ball still falling, and the creep read that residual as tension and slid the ring at ~7x the law.
Reading the creep against the ring held at the frame's start moved the residual 0.4 mm: it was the ball's, not the cord's.
As rows, the ball, the link and the cord are one velocity solve, nothing is rolled back, and the creep is the row's (`ring-hang` 3.04 cm/s against the law's 3.00).
On the session's own inputs the grab swings 0.84 m and the tip 1.45 m (0.49 and 0.75 positional), with the ball peaking at 3.5 m/s against 6.1; let go of mid-swing, ball and rope only lose energy, the free tip whipping to 7.5-9.9 m/s on the ball's.
A row's walk that runs off the cord's free end is answered at the top of the next frame (`Rope.settleRowRunOff`), as a positional ring's is at its first look; a span has no free end, so the island path never needed it before.
`ring-rope-haul` pins the rule (a light rope whirled under the ball: none of its share rolled back; 1940 mm without the island hold), and `ring-square`'s throw bar is the kick's own speed, since a cord that gives no longer cuts the kick's radial part on the first frame.

What is still unplayed: the push-fit ring, the thread-on inset, and the drop coming back armed.
