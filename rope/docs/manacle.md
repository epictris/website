# The manacle

The chain's far end is a manacle, and since 2026-09-08 it is a RING SEEN EDGE-ON: its axis lies in the gameplay plane, square to the chain, so what the camera sees and what the sim collides is the ring's silhouette - a bar as long as the ring is wide and as thick as the lock housing that stands proudest of its band (`lib/manacle.ts`: `manacleShape`, `MANACLE_LENGTH` by `MANACLE_THICKNESS`).
The chain is shackled to the HINGE PIN at one end of that bar (`MANACLE_HINGE` along the cuff's own +x) and the jaws meet under the lock at the other, which is the end that leads a throw and bites.
It was a disc lying in the plane, drawn flat-on with the chain ending at its centre, and the disc's face is the one thing a ring never shows a camera looking down its chain.

**The chain's end node is the hinge pin** - free, bitten or clamped - so the length the sim enforces is the length to the pin the links hang from, and the drawn chain ends where the physics does with nothing trimmed or lifted (`trimPathStart` went with the rim it trimmed to).
Free, the pin is a `RopeContact` on the hook body at that offset; bitten, it is a contact on the anchor body one ring radius from the bite along the cuff (`BallPlayer.onHookAttached`); around a rail the ring's centre stays the node and the drape is pinned to its rim, as before.
The flight budget (`deployLimit`) and the chain-out seat are measured to the pin too, and `BallPlayer.manaclePose` is the one answer both renderers draw from: centre, hinge direction, clamped or free, and the face the cuff is buried under.

A pin standing proud of the face is a chain end the wrap solver reaches from OUTSIDE its own shape, and the self-intersection resolvers - whose job the span's own endpoint shape is, since the scan excludes it - answered it as if it sat on the loop.
Their polygon rule walked to the vertex adjacent to the contact, which for a bite beside a vertex is the vertex 4.5 cm from the pin, bending the chain the wrong way; the cull dropped that wrap at the end of the same regeneration, after the scan had run with the rock excluded, so no path could see the corner the chain actually cut and the taut chain ran 8 cm through the rock for 150 frames while the drape was drawn stuck along its underside (`session-206f`, `chain-clip` from f59).
`Rope.ownCornerToWrap` now asks first whether the contact stands clear of its shape: if it does, the corner is the tangent vertex from the span's far end - the scan's own construction for a span through scenery, with the wrap sense flipped when the node is the span's start - and only a contact on the loop takes the adjacent-vertex walk.
`cli contacts` `chain-own-corner` holds both halves on the recording's numbers, and the bundle is in the corpus.

**The hook's rotation is driven, not integrated.** Every step turns the bar to face back along the chain (`BallHook.alignToChain`, off `chainEndFacing` over the sim's own path), the way a shackle on the end of a chain trails the chain, so the drawn cuff, the bar the sim collides and the hinge the chain is measured to cannot come apart.
It is told to the contact solver and the rope solver in the vocabulary they already have for the steered ball (`kinematicRotation`: infinite rotational inertia, no torque arm), so neither spends an impulse on a spin the next step overwrites.
A resting tip therefore lies at the chain's angle rather than flat, propped on its mouth corner with the hinge toward the ball; a manacle with the chain pulling at its pin is a light body in series between the ball and the ground and would want a rotational degree of freedom of its own, and `session-225f` is the record of trying that for the rail ring.

**The bar is swept as the loop it is** (`sweepConvex` in `engine/collision.ts`): the separating-axis sweep of a translating convex loop against a convex piece - in the plane the only axes that can separate two convex polygons are their face normals, and under a pure translation each axis's two intervals close at one exact time, so the first contact is the latest of those and the axis that closes last is the face they meet on.
Against a circle it is `sweepCirclePoly` run the other way round.
The attach sweep, the resting probe (`shapeContacts` at the probe margin) and `World.integrateContinuous` for a vertex-shaped body all go through it; the ball's own circle sweep is untouched, bit for bit.
Where the two loops TOUCH is the middle of the overlap of the two touching features along the axis's tangent, never one loop's feature alone: two parallel faces close at the same instant on both loops' normals, whichever was considered first wins, and read from the plank's face alone a manacle flying mouth-first into a 2.6 m plank was reported touching it at the plank's midpoint, 67 cm from the mouth - which is where the anchor then went (`plank-haul` went red on it).

**Reach is the cuff's own body.** The chain is budgeted to the hinge and an attach is forgiven nothing beyond it: the mouth leads the hinge by `MANACLE_MOUTH`, so a face the mouth can touch with the hinge at full stretch is bitten and one a hair further is not, on whatever frame the chain runs out (`hook-mouth-band`, `chain-out`).
The muzzle puts the MOUTH on the ball's rim: a muzzle any further out spawns the mouth past whatever thin surface the ball is resting against, and a hook aimed down at a slat the ball sits on has to meet that slat rather than appear underneath it (`hook-sparks`' drag rig is where that showed).

**A bite keeps the angle it arrived at.** The cuff is driven into the face like a spike, centred on the surface - the mouth half embedded, the hinge half standing proud - and stays at that angle for as long as it holds, unless it is the cuff's BACK that met the geometry (the hinge pointing into the surface, a tip lying hinge-down on a ledge), where no mouth bit anything and the cuff is set square to the face on the surface's own normal instead.
The 2D renderer buries the far half under the FACE's plane (`ManaclePose.buriedUnder`) rather than the cuff's own midline, since a cuff driven in at an angle has two halves that are not the two sides of the face; in 3D the geometry hides it.

**The clamped cuff is a solid piece of the body it bit** (`BallPlayer.mountCuff`: a `hidden`, non-wrappable `addShape` on the anchor body, unmounted with the chain): half of it stands proud of the face, and the ball is stopped by it exactly as by anything else bolted there.
Without it the ball wound all the way up to its anchor passed through the manacle to the face behind, where the hinge sat inside its own rim and the unwind's endgame - written to end with the anchor ON the rim - held 10 cm over length with the ball hauled up the face.
With it the ball meets the cuff first and, tethered, rides its spin up onto the bar until it sits in the POCKET between the cuff's outer top corner and the face, touching both, with the pin within the cuff's own thickness of its rim; `wound-tight` asserts that pocket as the ceiling of the climb.
A bite where the ball already stands mounts nothing: a point-blank throw bites the face at the ball's own contact, and a cuff appearing inside the ball would be a shove the throw never made.
The rail range and the corners a clamped span ignores still measure with the ring's bounding circle (`MANACLE_REACH`), which for a ring standing square on a bar is conservative by the difference between its length and its thickness: the ring stops that much short of a lid it slides toward, and closing that is a `RopeClamp` change for another day.
