# The manacle

The chain's far end is a manacle, and since 2026-09-08 it is a RING SEEN EDGE-ON: its axis lies in the gameplay plane, square to the chain, so what the camera sees and what the sim collides is the ring's silhouette - a bar as long as the ring is wide and as thick as its band is broad (`lib/manacle.ts`: `manacleShape`, `MANACLE_LENGTH` by `MANACLE_THICKNESS`, which is `MANACLE_WIDTH`).
Since 2026-10-13 the band is a leg iron's strap, 4.4 cm across - two fifths of the ring's diameter - where it was a ring of 1.7 cm round bar; the hinge knuckles, the lock plate and the rivets all stand inside the bar.
The chain is shackled to the HINGE PIN at one end of that bar (`MANACLE_HINGE` along the cuff's own +x) and the jaws meet under the lock at the other, which is the end that leads a throw and bites.
It was a disc lying in the plane, drawn flat-on with the chain ending at its centre, and the disc's face is the one thing a ring never shows a camera looking down its chain.

**The chain's end node is the hinge pin** - free, bitten or clamped - so the length the sim enforces is the length to the pin the links hang from, and the drawn chain ends where the physics does with nothing trimmed or lifted (`trimPathStart` went with the rim it trimmed to).
Free, the pin is a `RopeContact` on the hook body at that offset; bitten, it is a contact on the anchor body one ring radius from the bite along the cuff (`BallPlayer.onHookAttached`); around a rail the ring's centre stays the node and the drape is pinned to its rim, as before.
The flight budget (`deployLimit`) and the chain-out seat are measured to the pin too, and `BallPlayer.manaclePose` is the one answer both renderers draw from: centre, hinge direction, clamped or free, and the face the cuff is buried under.

A pin standing proud of the face is a chain end the wrap solver reaches from OUTSIDE its own shape, and the self-intersection resolvers - whose job the span's own endpoint shape is, since the scan excludes it - answered it as if it sat on the loop.
Their polygon rule walked to the vertex adjacent to the contact, which for a bite beside a vertex is the vertex 4.5 cm from the pin, bending the chain the wrong way; the cull dropped that wrap at the end of the same regeneration, after the scan had run with the rock excluded, so no path could see the corner the chain actually cut and the taut chain ran 8 cm through the rock for 150 frames while the drape was drawn stuck along its underside (`session-206f`, `chain-clip` from f59).
`Rope.ownCornerToWrap` now asks first whether the contact stands clear of its shape: if it does, the corner is the tangent vertex from the span's far end - the scan's own construction for a span through scenery, with the wrap sense flipped when the node is the span's start - and only a contact on the loop takes the adjacent-vertex walk.
`cli contacts` `chain-own-corner` holds both halves on the recording's numbers, and the bundle is in the corpus.

**The thrown hook's rotation is driven; the dangling cuff's is the physics'** (since 2026-11-09).
Through the throw every step turns the bar to face back along the chain (`BallHook.alignToChain`, off `chainEndFacing` over the sim's own path), so the hinge the flight is budgeted to and the bar the sweep flies cannot come apart; it is told to the contact solver and the rope solver in the vocabulary they already have for the steered ball (`kinematicRotation`: infinite rotational inertia, no torque arm).
When the throw ends (`endFlight`, which every way of becoming the dangling tip goes through) the cuff turns as the bar it is, with its own inertia: the chain's row pulls at the hinge pin with its torque arm, whatever it lies on pushes at its corners, gravity acts at its centre, and the island solves all three each substep and turns it (`ties.ts`).
It lands tumbling and settles flat, lies still, trails a chain that drags it, and tips up onto a corner and hangs from its pin as a chain hoists it.

Driven, it was given one of two poses - face the chain, or lie flat on whatever a 5 mm probe found under it (`lieFlat`, 2026-10-13 to 2026-11-09) - and the poses a cuff really takes are between them.
Lying flat, it was turned about its centre, lifted the corner it stood on past the probe, was snapped back to face the chain, and rocked 14 degrees each way at 30 Hz with the chain's end swinging 12.5 mm (`session-339f`; seven corpus bundles did the same).
Hoisted by its pin toward a ledge corner, a driven rotation could not tip it: the pull lifted the flat bar whole, the probe lost the floor, it was snapped 80 degrees to face the chain in one frame - its hinge, the chain's end, flicking 6-7 cm and the chain going that slack - then landed and was laid flat again, every five or six frames while the ball wound it in (`session-307f` f247-272, `session-256f` f191-201).
Pivoting the lie-down on its contact, bounding it by the chain's reach, and turning the snap about the hinge each cured one half and found the other's edge; turned by the physics, the same bundles keep the chain within 1.3 mm of its length through the hoist.
The detectors are `tip-rock` (a turn one way and straight back for more than six frames running) and `tip-hinge-flick` (the hinge moving further than the cuff's own linear and angular motion carry it), see [headless-tooling](headless-tooling.md).
`session-225f` is the record of a rotational degree of freedom for the rail ring going wrong - a light ring in series between a 52 kg ball and a 44 kg lantern, solved by three solvers that shared no Jacobian - and the dangling cuff is the other case: it hangs from one heavy body, the ball is an infinite mass to its row (`BallChainRow.dangling`), and the one island solves the row and the contacts together.

**The dangling cuff and the ball collide** (`BallPlayer.dangle`, 2026-11-09).
The throw carries a collision exception between the ball and its cuff, because it spawns the mouth on the rim, half inside; the frame the cuff becomes the dangling tip the exception goes, and from there the two are bodies that meet, two-way, as the ball meets a bridge slat: the cuff is a body of the chain's island and the ball (free while the chain dangles, see [ball-coil-and-hook](ball-coil-and-hook.md)) meets it through the island's contact rows with its real mass.
The ball rolls into its cuff and rides up over it, a cuff dropped on the ball rests on it - a bar on a circle is a stable rest - and the cuff hanging off the rim is held off the ball.
While the cuff was driven to face back along the chain, a cuff hung from the rim pointed away from the ball and the exception cost nothing; turned by the physics it swings about its hinge, and it swung into the ball and hung there, its centre 2.5 cm from the ball's and the bar 14 cm deep, for forty frames at a time with the chain at full length (`session-311f` f62-98, f136-158).
The one-way rule is the CHAIN's (`BallChainRow.dangling`): what the cuff is pinned against, held by or weighs does not reach the ball through the chain, which is what kept a 52 kg ball from winding itself up a wall on a tip pinned on a ledge (`session-373f`).
Touching the ball, the cuff is a body like any other - and within reach of the ball the chain is two-way too (`BallChainRow.tipOnBall`), because a two-way contact beside a one-way chain is momentum from nothing.
With the chain one-way, the winch hauled the cuff and nothing pulled the ball back, while the cuff pushed and dragged the ball: a ball standing on its cuff as the winch reeled it in was carried off at 3 m/s (`session-206f` f155-185), and one in `ball-roll-wall-dangling` drove itself 33 m along the floor.
Asked only of frames the two were touching, the winch whirled a short-chained cuff round the ball at 9 m/s on the frames between, paid for by nothing, and the cuff kicked the ball 22 cm up a wall; so it is asked of reach: the hinge within the bar's own span of the ball's surface (`CUFF_SPAN`, the furthest point of the bar from its hinge) plus what the two can close in a frame.
Out of reach the one-way rule stands, so a missed throw still cannot anchor the ball; what is left at that boundary is a cuff flung from beyond reach that swings back carrying speed its one-way frames gave it (chosen 2026-11-09 over a two-way chain throughout).
A one-way contact was tried first, the ball an infinite mass to a frictionless row on the cuff alone, and it held the open-air cases and lost every wedge: a ball rolling onto its cuff on the floor pressed it into the floor with nothing pushing back and sank 45 mm into it (`session-435f` f237-241), and a cuff in the crevice where a resting ball meets the floor sat 13 mm in (`session-1052f` f699-824).
Two-way, nothing in the corpus comes within a centimetre; rolled over its cuff standing on end, the ball rises 3.7 cm and the cuff never enters it.
A chain paid back out of the stow is born with the cuff hanging off the rim by its hinge, the bar pointing straight out of the ball (`BallPlayer.unstowIfUnwinding`); born facing back along the coil it lay along the rim with most of the bar inside the ball, 90 mm deep (`session-251f` f170).
Two cases that relied on the cuff falling through the ball (`attach-keeps-length`, `viscous-drop`) throw a little to the side, or roll out from under it, as a player would.
`tip-in-ball` is the detector: the dangling cuff more than a centimetre inside the ball, red on `session-311f` with the exception kept.

The driven dangling cuff's first fix was `lieFlat` itself: facing the chain alone, a tip lying still on the floor was turned after the ball's orbiting mounting loop every frame and rocked 15 degrees on its mouth corner (`session-391f`); a slack chain pulls on nothing, which the physics now says by itself.

**The bar is swept as the loop it is** (`sweepConvex` in `engine/collision.ts`): the separating-axis sweep of a translating convex loop against a convex piece - in the plane the only axes that can separate two convex polygons are their face normals, and under a pure translation each axis's two intervals close at one exact time, so the first contact is the latest of those and the axis that closes last is the face they meet on.
Against a circle it is `sweepCirclePoly` run the other way round.
The attach sweep, the resting probe (`shapeContacts` at the probe margin) and `World.integrateContinuous` for a vertex-shaped body all go through it; the ball's own circle sweep is untouched, bit for bit.
Where the two loops TOUCH is the middle of the overlap of the two touching features along the axis's tangent, never one loop's feature alone: two parallel faces close at the same instant on both loops' normals, whichever was considered first wins, and read from the plank's face alone a manacle flying mouth-first into a 2.6 m plank was reported touching it at the plank's midpoint, 67 cm from the mouth - which is where the anchor then went (`plank-haul` went red on it).

**Reach is the cuff's own body.** The chain is budgeted to the hinge and an attach is forgiven nothing beyond it: the mouth leads the hinge by `MANACLE_MOUTH`, so a face the mouth can touch with the hinge at full stretch is bitten and one a hair further is not, on whatever frame the chain runs out (`hook-mouth-band`, `chain-out`).
The muzzle puts the MOUTH on the ball's rim: a muzzle any further out spawns the mouth past whatever thin surface the ball is resting against, and a hook aimed down at a slat the ball sits on has to meet that slat rather than appear underneath it (`hook-sparks`' drag rig is where that showed).

**A bite keeps the angle it arrived at.** The cuff is driven into the face like a spike, centred on the surface - the mouth half embedded, the hinge half standing proud - and stays at that angle for as long as it holds, unless it is the cuff's BACK that met the geometry (the hinge pointing into the surface, a tip lying hinge-down on a ledge), where no mouth bit anything and the cuff is set square to the face on the surface's own normal instead.
The 2D renderer buries the far half under the FACE's plane (`ManaclePose.buriedUnder`) rather than the cuff's own midline, since a cuff driven in at an angle has two halves that are not the two sides of the face; in 3D the geometry hides it.

**The clamped cuff is a solid piece of the body it bit** (`BallPlayer.mountCuff`: a `hidden`, non-wrappable `addShape` on the anchor body, unmounted with the chain): half of it stands proud of the face, and the ball is stopped by it exactly as by anything else bolted there.
Without it the ball wound all the way up to its anchor passed through the manacle to the face behind, where the hinge sat inside its own rim and the unwind's endgame - written to end with the anchor ON the rim - held 10 cm over length with the ball hauled up the face.
With it the ball meets the cuff first and, tethered, rides its spin up onto the bar until it sits in the POCKET between the cuff's outer top corner and the face, touching both, with the pin within the cuff's own thickness of its rim; `wound-tight` asserts that pocket as the ceiling of the climb.
A bite where the ball already stands mounts nothing: a point-blank throw bites the face at the ball's own contact, and a cuff appearing inside the ball would be a shove the throw never made.
The rail range and the corners a clamped span ignores still measure with the ring's bounding circle (`MANACLE_REACH`), which for a ring standing square on a bar is conservative by the difference between its length and its thickness: the ring stops that much short of a lid it slides toward, and closing that is a `RopeClamp` change for another day.
