# Rails

**A rail is an authored CURVE with a width** (`ShapeData`'s `curve` plus `CollisionObjectData.rail`, `lib/stroke.ts` + `lib/rail.ts`): a thin bar the manacle clamps AROUND rather than bites into, and then slides along under the chain's pull against the body's `friction`.
A zipline, a pipe, the handles of a hanging lantern - which is the case the flag is per SHAPE for: the lantern's handles are rails and its lid, bulb and base are hook-proof, in one body.
Hook-proof wins where both are set (the sweep sorts a piece as hook-proof before it is anything else), and the bar is solid for everything but the hook.

Only a curve may be one, and that is the whole of where a rail's geometry comes from: **the cuff rides the line the author drew.**
The flag was once offered on any shape and the centreline was derived - a rect's medial axis, a polygon's principal axis, a circle's centre - which is a guess at a line nobody drew, and it cannot express the thing a handle IS: `levels/ball.json`'s lantern handles were authored as ten-vertex polygons whose principal axis is a straight line through a curved bar.
Set on a rect, a circle or a polygon the flag is now ignored.

**The engine has no curved primitive and is never getting one**, so a curve is STROKED at load (`strokeCurve`) into the convex pieces that tile it - the same statement `decomposeConvex` makes about a concave outline, and the same load-time cut.
One quad per segment of the centreline, sharing a **mitred** edge at each joint, plus a bevel triangle wherever the mitre would spike past `STROKE_MITER_LIMIT` (a 120° turn).
The tiling is exact - no overlaps, no gaps - so the piece areas sum to the bar's and their masses to its mass, and `mountPieces`, `setCompoundInertia` and `isExposedCorner` all read it as the ordinary compound body it is.
Past the build nothing knows it was ever a curve.

**One polyline answers everything.** The cuff rides `line`, the pieces are built from `line`, and the drawn outline is offset from `line`, so the bar that is drawn, the bar that is collided and the bar the ring slides along cannot disagree by a pixel - which is why the flattening and its simplification happen once, in `lib/stroke.ts`, rather than per consumer.
`flattenPath` samples by control-polygon length (25 cm, the camera path's own step) and a Douglas-Peucker pass then drops the samples that buy nothing, at `STROKE_TOLERANCE` - a centimetre, a tenth of the manacle's disc, capped at a fifth of the half-width so a thin bar is not straightened by a tolerance the size of its own thickness.
A straight bar is therefore ONE piece however many nodes it was clicked out with, and a gentle four-metre bow is a handful rather than the twenty the flattener produces.

**The clamp is a `RopeAttachment` whose contact can move** (`RopeClamp`), on the rail's own body, so the anchored regime is untouched: a rail on a static is a fixed anchor, a rail on the hanging lantern is a lever on the lantern, and the winch, the credits, the refusals and the lease all read it as the anchor it is.
The hook body is removed on clamping exactly as on a bite.
Where the cuff stands is `s` metres of ARC LENGTH along the curve, and every question about it - the point at `s`, the tangent at `s`, where the bite projects onto the line - is `lib/path.ts`'s, the module the camera paths and the movers' routes already ride.
`RailCurve` (declared in `engine/shapes.ts`, which may not import from `lib/`, with `PolylineIndex`'s field names so a curve is passed straight to those functions) is that index in the BODY's local frame plus the half-width and which piece covers each segment; **every piece the stroke produced holds the same object**, so a hook that strikes any of them finds the whole bar, and the contact's `shapeIndex` follows from where the cuff stands rather than being stored beside it.
What the clamp adds to the solve is one step at the top of every iteration of `Rope.correctShapePositionAndRotation` (`slideClampedEnd`), before the bodies split what is left of the error.

**The cuff is massless**, so it is solved geometrically rather than integrated: a quarter-kilo ring under a fifty-kilo ball's pull is wherever force balance puts it within a frame, and force balance for a ring on a bar is the friction cone.
`slideStep` splits the last span's pull against the rail tangent; inside `mu·across` the ring is held, outside it runs toward the ball's plumb by `|along| − mu_k·across`, which is the point the pull meets the kinetic cone's edge.
The static coefficient holds a stuck ring and the kinetic one a running one, and **the friction state is decided once a frame, on the solve's first look at the clamp**: a running ring whose pull has come back inside the kinetic cone since the last frame has stopped being driven and is stuck from there; one still outside it runs to the edge again.
Later iterations of the same frame find the ring AT the edge and must not read that as rest, or a ring under a ball driving it steadily re-sticks and breaks away every frame - a stick-slip judder at frame rate, measured as the lean sawtoothing 16.9° to 19.3° where a steady 16.7° is the physics.
Written this way the closed forms come out exact: on a frictionless bar the chain stays plumb to 0.00° and a 4 m/s ball coasts at 4.000 m/s; with grip the chain trails at atan(mu_k) to a tenth of a degree and the ball stops within Coulomb's `v²/(2·mu_k·g)`.
A slide may leave the chain SLACK - the ring at the cone's edge can be nearer the ball than a span - and that is the physics: a ball swung wide past the static cone is released from its arc and caught when the chain comes taut again.
On a BEND the step is re-solved against the direction the bar runs where the ring has got to (up to `SLIDE_STEPS` looks), so the cone is a statement about the bar rather than about where the ring set off from; there is no joint and no hop, a bend being one curve with collision pieces under it the ring never has to know about.
How far the ring may run in a frame is bounded by what is DRIVING it (`slideClampedEnd`): the speed it already had along the bar, the speed of the node pulling it measured along the bar, and one frame of gravity on top, capped by `RAIL_MAX_SLIDE_SPEED` (the hook's own 12 m/s).
So a ring under a ball falling down a vertical bar falls with the ball at `g`, a ring under a coasting zipline ball keeps pace with it exactly, and a ring a ball has swung past the static cone slips away at the ball's own speed rather than leaping to the cone's edge in a frame and dropping the ball off its arc - which a fixed 12 m/s budget did, and which read as a teleport.
The budget is per frame and is part of the solve's monotone-guard snapshot, as is the clamp's arc length, so an undone iteration puts the ring back and refunds its road.
`RAIL_STATIC_FRICTION`/`RAIL_KINETIC_FRICTION` (1.5 / 1.4, rubber on steel - which is what `friction: 1` means for every other surface) scale by the body's `friction` exactly as a rigid body's contact friction does, and both are guesses to be played; `cli rails` pins the LAW against the coefficient rather than the coefficient.
What the pair actually sets is the angle the pull may make with the rail's NORMAL before the cuff gives way, the cone test being a pure direction: 56° static and 54.5° kinetic at these values, and a hanging ball's own weight is already off the normal by the rail's slope.
They started at 0.35 / 0.3 and then 0.8 / 0.6, and both read as no grip: at 19° every ordinary swing of a 0.6 m chain crept the cuff 15-20 cm along a friction-1 bar (`session-526f`), and at 31° a 30° slope was the balance point, so a ball hanging still rode the cuff down the whole bar at 1 m/s (`session-212f`).
The gap between the two is kept small on purpose: a breakaway runs the cuff from the static cone to the kinetic one within a frame and frees `across·(1/cos a_s − 1/cos a_k)` of chain doing it - 8% of the span at this pair, against the 43% a 2.0 / 1.5 pair would drop the ball by.

**The cuff is a RING, not a pin**: its inside RESTS on the bar at one point and everything else about it hangs from that point, the way a chain link laid over a rod does.
So the pull does not shift the cuff sideways, it SWINGS it (`RopeClamp.seat`): the point it rests on stays where it is on the bar - the surface square across from where the ring stands, `s` being that contact's own arc length - and the cuff's centre, which is where the chain ends and where the drawn ring is centred, travels the arc of its own bore about it.
A chain leaning `a` off the bar's normal therefore carries the cuff `R·sin a` along the bar and leaves it `R·cos a` under the contact, `R` being the bore's radius; a ring spinning about its own centre instead was the tell that this was missing.
What stops the swing is the bar's own THICKNESS: a ring of inner radius `R` tilted by `a` presents an aperture of `R·cos a` across the bar, so it tilts until that aperture is the bar's half-width (`cos a_max = h/R`, `RopeClamp.tiltMax`) and then JAMS - the chain may go on past that angle, the ring does not follow it.
A wire lets it hang almost along itself; a bar as wide as the bore is a push fit that never tilts at all and the ring sits square on it.

**Massless to the ball is not the same as stateless.** Three things about the ring are its own and are carried from frame to frame rather than re-derived from whatever the chain happens to be doing: which SURFACE of the bar it rests on (`side`, decided once when the hook threads on - the surface away from the side the hook arrived from - and never changed), how far it has TILTED on that surface (`tilt`), and how fast it is RUNNING along the bar of its own accord (`speed`).
Re-deriving the hang from the pull every frame was the version before, and it had the failures a derived quantity always has: a facing with a sign rule that flipped end for end as the pull crossed the bar, a ring that tilted to lie flat along a bar it should have jammed on, and no answer at all when the chain was slack - and the attempt to fix those by making the manacle a real rigid body threaded on the bar (two face discs a bore apart, contacts against the bar) was a light body in series between a 52 kg ball and a 44 kg lantern, solved by three solvers a frame that shared no Jacobian, and it juggled both (`session-225f`: the length solve turned a 14 cm attach snap into 4 m/s on the lantern, then the ring sat at its spin cap for thirty-frame runs while the lantern's spin swung ±9 rad/s).
A state cannot flip between frames, and it costs nothing.
The tilt follows the pull - or gravity, when the chain was not pulling last frame - at no more than `RAIL_TILT_RATE` (12 rad/s, a guess to be played: well above any rate a swing turns the pull at, so in play the ring follows the chain exactly and the bound only shows on a throw or a catch), clamped to `tiltMax`.
**The ring never swaps sides of the bar.** A design decision (2026-09-08): the first version flipped the ring to the other surface when the pull came squarely from the one it rested on - what a bead free to roll round its wire would do, and on a near-vertical lantern handle a ball swinging underneath did it on every swing - and the drawn ring's long axis turned by twice the tilt in a single frame each time (`session-395f`).
Rolling it round over nine frames instead was tried and rejected the same evening: the ring is a ring on this side of the bar for as long as it is threaded.
A pull from the far side of the bar leans it as far as the jam lets it toward the pull's run along the bar, a pull straight up through the bar leaves the tilt where it is, and the chain bends.
It also cannot lean INTO an end that ends in something: at a closed end (a lid, a post, the lantern's base) the rest point stops the ring's reach `R·sin a` short of the range's end and the tilt toward it is bounded by the room left (`restMax`), so the ring's far side touches the lid and no more; at an open end, and on a peg, it may lean out.
The hang is settled once a frame in `settleClamp`, outside the length solve and before the path is regenerated, because a ring hangs whether or not the chain is taut - a ball rolling about on the floor under a slack chain still swings the cuff, and the length solve returns before it ever looks at a clamp when there is no error to correct.
"Pulling" is read from a slack the ring's own hang cannot move: the last span is measured to the point the ring RESTS on rather than to its swung centre, which is a function of `s` alone.
The two answers hang the ring in opposite directions - the node the chain reaches it from, or straight down - so a signal the hang contaminates drives itself, which is what the first version did: the ring swung, the swing moved the chain's end by up to a bore's DIAMETER against a margin of a bore's radius, the moved end crossed the threshold, and the ring swung back, every frame.
The centre stands exactly a bore's radius from the rest point whatever the tilt is, so `MANACLE_BORE / 2` on the rest-measured slack is the EXACT bound rather than a margin: within it the chain can be taut at some hang of the ring, and slack by more than the ring itself can make is a chain that has really gone slack.
That slack is also the WHOLE test: a `loaded` term beside it - the length solve moved this ring last frame, so the chain must be pulling on it - is a second opinion about the same thing, and where the ring sits near the bound the two disagree, the solve touching it every third or fourth frame and each of those frames hanging the ring the other way, so the drawn ring flicked 11° and back for as long as the chain took to come taut (`session-164f`).
And the span that ends on the ring ignores every CORNER of the bar's body inside the cuff's own disc - in the wrap scan, in the start-of-span resolver, and as a standing cull of wraps that have come inside it (`inCuff` in `regeneratePath` and `resolveSelfIntersectionAtStart`, `cullNodesInCuff`, all three asking `RopeClamp.covers`): the chain leaves the ring at its rim, so the joint of the bar the ring straddles and the near corner of the lid it hangs beside are metal the chain is already clear of, not corners to bend round - wrapping the lid 12 mm from the ring's centre gave a last span shorter than the ring is wide, whose direction the ring's own tilt changed, and the ring hunted between that and gravity while the drawn cuff jumped about the handle (`session-189f`); the corner and not the piece, because the far corner of that same base is what the chain bends round when the ball winds up under the lantern (`session-154f`).
The disc is measured from the point the ring RESTS on and by the ring's reach PLUS its swing, which is where the ring's metal can be at any hang and so does not depend on the tilt.
Taken from the ring's CENTRE it swung with the ring, and born-and-forgotten is not a rule but a race: a corner born 54 mm out - the reach alone is 53.5 - was 2 mm from the centre eight frames later, and a last span two millimetres long has no direction but noise, so the ring hunted a fifth of a radian either way of it at frame rate for the rest of the recording (`session-153f`).
The slack drape applies the same rule (`SlackChain.solveCollisions`): its nodes inside the cuff's disc are not pushed out of the bar's body, since the ring's centre stands millimetres off the bar's surface and pushing them shoved the last links off the handle every step, a twitch at the cuff for as long as the ball hung still (`session-291f`).

**While nothing is pulling, the ring is a bead on a wire** (`RopeClamp.coast`, in the same once-a-frame step): it accelerates at gravity's component along the bar, held by Coulomb friction on gravity's component across it with the same coefficients the cone uses, so a ring threaded onto a vertical bar falls at `g` from the frame it is threaded (the ball standing on the floor under it loads the chain by nothing) and a ring on a horizontal bar lies where it was put.
While the chain IS pulling, the solve says where the ring stands and `coast` only observes the speed that came to, so the ring is not stopped dead the moment the chain goes slack: a ring on a zipline keeps coasting.
**A TAUT chain blocks the bead, one way along the bar.**
The rope's length is an inequality, so what it forbids is the motion that opens it further, and the ring's own weight may not make that motion (`Rope.clampHang` hands `coast` the direction; moving the ring by `ds` changes the last span by `-(p̂·t)·ds`, so the path lengthens toward increasing arc length exactly when the pull runs back against it).
Unblocked, two friction models ran on the same ring in the same frame - this one against gravity as though nothing held it, and `slide` against the pull as though gravity were not there - so the ring crept down the bar for as long as the cone held it and was hauled back the moment the pull leaned out of the cone: a permanent 3 mm sawtooth at a quarter of frame rate, in the chain's own anchor, which is the whole chain shivering (`session-153f`).
It is a DIRECTION and not a flag because taut is not held: a ring on a vertical bar with the ball standing on the floor below it has a chain at its full length and no tension in it at all, and it falls the length of the bar, because falling SHORTENS that chain's path.
**The speed it observes is what the ring did over the WHOLE frame** - the step it coasted plus the correction the solve then made to it - and not the correction alone over `dt`.
A constraint may only take motion out, so a chain going taut ARRESTS a coasting ring; measured from the post-coast mark instead, the arrest read as a shove back up the bar exactly as large as the coast had been, and that is self-sustaining: the ring ran down the bar, the chain caught it, the catch launched it back up, and it ran down again.
Sixteen frames and 8 cm of it, for the rest of the recording, on the lantern's handle in `session-283f` - which is what the ring jittering along its rail was - and it also halved a zipline ring's own speed in the steady state, the solve making up the other half every frame.
`cli rails` `rail-catch` pins both: a catch that puts the ring back reads as zero, one that takes back half the step leaves half its speed, a solve that drives the ring further than it coasted is the whole of the motion, an uncaught ring still falls at `g`, and a taut chain stops the ring the way that would open it and says nothing about the way that closes it.

**The range** runs from a half-width in from each end of the curve that ends in something, and to the very end of one that ends in the air, and is clipped at clamp time by sweeping the manacle's bounding circle (`MANACLE_REACH`) along the curve, segment by segment, against every piece of the body that is NOT part of this bar, so the cuff stops where it meets the lantern's lid - conservatively, by the difference between the ring's length and its thickness, since the ring on a bar stands square to it (see [**The manacle**](manacle.md)).
Pieces of the same curve do not clip (they are the bar) and other BODIES do not clip at all - the hook body that would have collided with them is gone - so a rail run through another body's wall is a level-design mistake.
Whether an END is open is asked of every body, though (`railEndIsOpen`): a lantern's bail ends inside its lid, a zipline strung between two posts ends inside the posts, whatever bodies they are, and a bar that ends in the air is OPEN there.
A ring that reaches an open end - run there by its own weight, or driven there by the ball - runs OFF it: `coast` reports it, and `BallPlayer.dropFromRail` turns the ring back into the dangling chain tip, spawned just clear of the bar's end face at the speed it was being driven at (`overrun`, what the end refused the last slide, not the speed the end let it have), DISARMED - a manacle that has slid off a rail is a weight on the end of the chain until it is thrown again, rather than something that re-catches the bar's end on the frame after it left it.
A bar shorter than it is thick has no room for the cuff to stand anywhere but its middle, and that is what a PEG is: the range collapses to a point and the ring hangs there and pivots without sliding.

The end's own piece is excluded from `resolveSelfIntersectionAtEnd`, since the contact is in the bar's bore by construction - inside the bar for a push fit, a centimetre under it for a thin one - and that resolver reads a span ending inside its own piece as the chain having wound around its anchor; the wrap scan already excludes the span's own end shape, and sibling pieces are scanned as ordinary scenery, so the chain still wraps the lid - and still bends round the outside of a curved rail, which is what a chain lying along a bend does.
`BallHook`'s attach callbacks are handed the PIECE the hook reached (the sweep's hit, the blocking contact's constraint, the probe's nearest piece) rather than re-deriving it from the point, because at the joint between a handle and its lid the nearest surface to the bite can be either.

Rendering: a rail wears a steel line down the bar - the authored curve itself, drawn once per CURVE rather than once per piece - in the game's 2D renderer and in the editor (the 3D scene draws the authored form and needs nothing), and a clamped cuff is drawn as the edge-on ring every cuff now is, with the bar through its bore and nothing about it buried (`ManaclePose.onRail`).
Its facing is THE WAY IT HANGS: the end of the ring the chain leaves over, where the hinge is (`RopeClamp.rimLocal`, through `manacleFacing` so both renderers get the same answer).
A cuff that bit a face is bolted to it and keeps the facing it bit with, but one on a rail is bolted to nothing, and a ring resting on a bar hangs in the plane of what is pulling it - a curtain ring on its rail - so it pivots on the bar as the ball swings and hangs plumb under a sloped bar rather than standing square to it.
Read live off the clamp rather than from the frozen `anchorFacingLocal`, which on a curved handle is the way the ring hung where the hook first STRUCK it.
Because the sim hangs the ring where the drawing shows it, the drawn ring's inner edge lands exactly on the bar's surface and its centre is exactly the chain's own end node: nothing about the cuff is fudged at draw time.
The chain's last link is hooked over the END of the ring the hinge is on rather than run to the drape's own direction: a cuff seen edge-on has metal at the two ends of its long axis and hole everywhere between them, so a link laid at the chain's direction from the centre is a link attached to the air inside the ring.
Which end is the clamp's own state (`rimSign`, the end that runs toward the sim's pull), and the slack drape is PINNED there (`RopeClamp.rimPoint`, `SlackChain.step`) rather than at the ring's centre and re-pointed at draw time: re-pointed, the first link ran from the rim back toward a drape node a bore's radius from the centre, and where a ring jammed against the lantern's base held its tilt short of a chain pulling along the bar, that link was drawn 30° wrong and flickered with the node (`session-407f`).
The rim is decided with **hysteresis**, and only while the chain is pulling (`RIM_FLIP_LEAN`, 0.25 - the sine of about 15°, a guess to be played): the chain re-hooks over the other end of the ring only once the pull has leaned that far past square to the ring's axis toward it, and the gravity stand-in `settleClamp` seats a slack ring toward never moves it.
Read as a bare sign it flickered: a ring jammed square against the lantern's base with the chain running along the handle has its pull within a few thousandths of square, so the sign was noise (+0.008 one frame, -0.004 another), and the `pulling` flag toggling on `MANACLE_BORE/2` of slack swapped the seat target between the ball and straight down, which lie on opposite sides of the ring's axis - seven hops of the ring's whole length in two hundred frames, read from the game as the chain flipping from one side of the cuff to the other (`session-821f`, f632-f788).
The ring free to tilt follows the pull to within a frame or two, which puts the lean near 1 and well clear of the band, so the band only ever shows on a jammed ring - the one that should keep its end.
`cli rails` `rail-rim` pins the law rather than the number: no flip inside the band on either side of square, a flip past it, none from a slack chain.
Both renderers therefore leave a rail path's end alone and centre the cuff on the chain's own end node (`chain.end.contact.renderGlobalPosition`).
The editor's `+ Curve` is the camera path's own tool and gestures - a run of clicks, nodes dragged, tangent grips bowed, a midpoint clicked to insert one, Alt+click to remove one - with the bar's `width` in the inspector beside a live piece count, and a freshly drawn curve is a rail (unticking the box makes it a plain curved wall).
The checkbox is offered on a curve alone and is mutually exclusive with hook-proof.
`cli rails` (`sim/railCases.ts`) is the coverage: the stroke's arithmetic (the pieces tile the bar exactly, every one convex, the line within tolerance of the cubic, a right angle mitred and a hairpin bevelled), the cone arithmetic, the clamp on a bar and on a peg, the ring resting on the bar's surface a bore's radius from the cuff and hanging square with `tiltMax` at `acos(h/R)`, the cuff pivoting on that point by `R·sin a` while the ring itself does not move, stuck inside the static cone, the frictionless coast, Coulomb's braking, the lid stopping the cuff and bouncing the hook, a ring threaded onto a vertical bar falling at `g` and landing on the block at its foot, a ring driven off an open end coming back as the dangling tip clear of the bar and at speed, the jam (pulled along the bar the tilt stops at `tiltMax`; reversed, it turns by one frame's rate; pulled straight up through the bar, the ring stays on its side of it; pulled along the bar, it does not flip), the catch (a chain going taut arrests the ring rather than launching it), the end of the ring the chain leaves over (holds inside `RIM_FLIP_LEAN` of square, follows a committed pull, ignores slack), the ring running round a bend, and the curve through the format, the build and the editor.
`RAIL_TEST` (`levels/rail-test.json`) is the sandbox: a slick zipline between two posts, a peg, a sloped bar, and the lantern on a chain with two curved handles.
